# Wameir Writing Style Guide

The voice for all copy on wameir.com. Check new or edited copy against the
[final checklist](#final-checklist) before it ships.

## Brand voice overview

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Tone** | Confident, direct and human |
| **Perspective** | First-person plural ("we") for the company; second person ("you") when addressing readers |
| **Energy** | Grounded, committed, forward-looking |
| **Avoid** | AI-generated patterns, corporate jargon, hedging language, defensive phrasing |

## Core principles

### 1. Direct and confident language

- Write with authority and clarity.
- Use active voice and strong verbs.
- Remove filler words and unnecessary qualifiers.
- State commitments clearly, without hedging.

> **Good:** "We use our own capital to acquire companies."
>
> **Bad:** "We are looking to potentially utilize our financial resources..."

### 2. Human, conversational tone

- Sound like a real person speaking, not an AI or a corporate document.
- Use natural sentence rhythms with varied lengths.
- Keep it professional but approachable.
- Avoid overly formal or stiff phrasing.

> **Good:** "We work hands-on with your team to preserve and grow your legacy."
>
> **Bad:** "We facilitate collaborative partnerships to ensure sustainable growth trajectories."

### 3. First-person plural ("we") consistency

- Always use "we" when referring to the company.
- Never hide behind third-person language like "an owner who stays" or "a founder who sells".
- Make "we" the clear subject of statements about actions and commitments.

> **Good:** "When we stay, we invest in what pays off slowly."
>
> **Bad:** "An owner who stays is the one who lives with the results."

### 4. Second-person engagement ("you")

- Address readers directly as "you".
- Focus on their experience, needs and perspective.
- Balance "we" statements with "you" engagement.

> **Good:** "You get to relax and enjoy a job well done."
>
> **Bad:** "Founders can expect to experience relief upon transition completion."

## What to avoid

### AI-style patterns

- **No em dashes.** Use commas, periods or colons instead.
- **No hedging phrases**, such as:
  - "We are writing this down because..."
  - "We've tried to explain..."
  - "We intend to..." (when unnecessary)
  - "That's why we started..."
- **No defensive language.** Don't justify or over-explain.
- **No vague metaphors**, such as:
  - "Earn that page"
  - "Magic solutions"
  - "Minding the store"

### Structural issues

- Avoid repetitive sentence patterns.
- Don't use colon-lists for simple ideas ("the team, the service, the software").
- No unnecessary subheaders when a single clear statement works.
- Avoid circular or redundant explanations.

### Clichés and overused phrases

| Avoid | Instead |
| --- | --- |
| "Done right" | A specific value statement |
| "Minding the store" | Say specifically what you mean |
| "Magic solutions" | Describe the actual mechanism |
| "We're not leaving" | "We build for the long term" |

## Tone by audience

### Founders and sellers

- Respectful and serious about their legacy.
- Clear about what they can expect.
- Emphasize preservation of value and a smooth transition.
- Phrases that fit:
  - "Your legacy continues with stronger support"
  - "Now you get to relax and enjoy a job well done"
  - "Feel confident leaving your company in good hands"

### Communities and boards

- Focus on tangible benefits: saving money, saving time, a better experience.
- Be specific about commitments.
- Avoid vague promises; be honest about limitations.
- Emphasize transparency and accountability.
- Phrases that fit:
  - "Every goal must save households money or time"
  - "We are committed to delivering tangible value"
  - "The reasoning behind a commitment matters just as much as the commitment itself"

### General brand voice

- Confident but not arrogant.
- Honest about what we can and cannot control.
- Forward-looking and committed to continuous improvement.
- Grounded in action, not just words.

## Sentence examples

**Good**

- "We use our own capital and funds from partners who know us well."
- "Every goal on our list must save households money or time."
- "We work hands-on with your team to preserve and grow your legacy."
- "You get a prompt response every time we receive your inquiry."

**Bad**

- "We are writing this down anyway, because the reasoning behind a promise matters as much as the promise, and because we want to be held to it."
- "An owner who stays is the one who lives with the results. So we spend on what pays off over years..."
- "The hardest part of selling is not knowing how it goes. So here's the entire path in detail..."
- "Technology that disappears. If you notice it, we've done it wrong."

## Key phrases to use instead

| Instead of | Use |
| --- | --- |
| "We are writing this down because" | Start directly with the principle or statement |
| "We intend to stay" | "We build for the long term" |
| "We've tried to explain" | "We have provided clear reasoning" |
| "An owner who stays invests..." | "When we stay, we invest..." |
| "Technology that disappears" | "Technology that empowers" or "stays out of your way" |
| "Done right" | "With integrity" or a specific value statement |
| "We're not leaving" | "We build for the long term" |
| "Handing over your name" | "Handing over your reputation" |

## Paragraph structure

1. Start with a clear, direct statement.
2. Follow with supporting details that add value.
3. End with a forward-looking or action-oriented sentence.

Keep each paragraph focused on one idea, and use transitions that feel natural,
not forced.

> "We work hands-on with your team to preserve and grow your legacy for years to
> come. Your company keeps its name, its people, and its community presence while
> receiving the investment needed to become stronger over time. We bought it to
> run it, so you can feel confident leaving your company in good hands."

## Page-specific guidelines

### Home

- The hero statement should be clear about what Wameir does.
- Avoid vague slogans; be specific about the value proposition.
- Use "with integrity" instead of "done right".
- Focus on action-oriented language.

### Approach

- Section titles should be clear and meaningful.
- Use "Our commitment" instead of "Why we stay".
- Technology should "empower", not "disappear".
- Emphasize augmenting people, not replacing them.

### Working toward

- State goals as commitments, not aspirations.
- Lead with tangible benefits (money saved, time saved).
- Be honest about limitations and external factors.
- Close with a forward-looking commitment to continuous improvement.

### Selling

- Respect founders' legacy and concerns.
- Explain the process clearly, without hedging.
- Emphasize preservation of value.
- Use phrases that build confidence in the transition.

### Team

- Focus on relevant experience and achievements.
- Connect individual expertise to the company mission.
- Keep bios concise but substantive.
- Avoid unnecessary qualifiers like "consultant who".

## Final checklist

Before publishing any copy, verify:

- [ ] No em dashes
- [ ] "We" used consistently for company actions
- [ ] Readers addressed directly as "you"
- [ ] No AI-style hedging or defensive language
- [ ] Clear, specific value statements instead of vague promises
- [ ] Natural sentence rhythm and varied structure
- [ ] Professional but approachable tone
- [ ] Forward-looking commitment language
- [ ] Honest about limitations where they apply
- [ ] No clichés or overused phrases
